## The facts (sources below)
- Annie Edson Taylor, a schoolteacher, went over the Canadian Horseshoe Falls in a barrel on 24 October 1901, her
  63rd birthday (born 24 October 1838). She claimed to be much younger: she gave 1860 as her birth year (about 42)
  (Wikipedia). Narration: "She tells everyone she's twenty years younger."
- Motive: by 1900 she had "fallen upon hard times" (her home burned, money lost in an investment) and wanted financial
  security from the fame (Wikipedia). Narration: "She's broke, and she thinks this stunt will make her rich."
- "First person to survive a trip over Niagara Falls in a barrel" (Wikipedia). Narration: "Nobody has ever gone over
  in a barrel and survived."
- Barrel: oak and iron, lined with a mattress; friends used "a bicycle tire pump to compress the air in the barrel"
  (Wikipedia). The barrel was towed out into the river and cut loose above the falls; narration: "set her adrift".
- Cat test: two days before (22 October 1901) a cat was sent over in the barrel; it survived (found with a bleeding
  head) and posed with Taylor in photographs (Wikipedia). Narration: "The cat survived." (no injury shown).
- Rescue: the trip took "less than twenty minutes"; she was "relatively uninjured, except for a small gash on her
  head" (Wikipedia). Narration: "About twenty minutes later, rescuers grab the barrel. She climbs out with a cut on her
  head, alive."
- Her words afterwards: "If it was with my dying breath, I would caution anyone against attempting the feat... I would
  sooner walk up to the mouth of a cannon, knowing it was going to blow me to pieces than make another trip over the
  Fall." Also reported: "I will never go over the Falls again." Narration: "Her verdict? Never again." (The popular
  line "No one ought ever do that again" could not be confirmed word for word, so it is not quoted.)
- Manager: Frank M. Russell "ran away with her barrel"; it was found later in Chicago (and stolen again later); "most
  of her savings were used towards private detectives" searching for it; she earned little and died penniless in
  1921 (Wikipedia). Narration: "Then her manager runs off with her barrel. She spends her savings on detectives, just
  to get it back."
- Staging only: who plays whom, the dock, the birthday cake, the age card, the money thought bubble, the boat, the
  newspaper, the poster.

8. Rescue: below, Skye in a rowboat hooks the barrel; "20 MIN LATER".
9. Alive: the lid pops, Mia climbs out with a bandage, dizzy then smiles: "ALIVE!"; bubble "NEVER AGAIN!".
10. Famous: newspaper "SHE DID IT!"; crowd; Max (manager, top hat) beams.
11. Payoff: Max rolls the barrel away down the road, sneaky; Mia shocked. Then Mia pays coins to a detective
    (Noob) under a "LOST: ONE BARREL" poster; "SAVINGS: $0". CTA end card.
