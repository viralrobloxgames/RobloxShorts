```
On 24 October 1901, her 63rd birthday, Annie Edson Taylor, a schoolteacher, became the first person to survive going over Niagara Falls in a barrel. She had fallen on hard times and hoped the stunt would bring her fame and money, and she told everyone she was in her forties.

Two days earlier, a cat was sent over the Horseshoe Falls in her barrel as a test; it survived. On the day, friends used a bicycle pump to pressurize the air inside the oak-and-iron barrel, which was lined with a mattress, sealed it, and set it adrift in the river above the falls. She went over the Canadian Horseshoe Falls and was pulled out less than twenty minutes later, alive, with a small cut on her head.

Afterwards she said: "If it was with my dying breath, I would caution anyone against attempting the feat... I would sooner walk up to the mouth of a cannon, knowing it was going to blow me to pieces than make another trip over the Fall."

The fortune never came. Her manager, Frank M. Russell, ran off with the barrel, and she spent most of her savings on private detectives to track it down (it was found in Chicago). She died in 1921 with almost no money.
```
