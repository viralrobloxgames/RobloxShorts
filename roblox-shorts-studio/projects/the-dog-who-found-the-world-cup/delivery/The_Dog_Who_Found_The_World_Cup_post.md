In March 1966, four months before England hosted the World Cup, the Jules Rimet Trophy was stolen from a guarded glass cabinet at a stamp exhibition in Westminster Central Hall, London. A ransom demand for £15,000 followed; police arrested a man at the handover, but he said he was only a middleman, and the trophy stayed missing.

On Sunday 27 March 1966, a week after the theft, David Corbett was walking his black-and-white collie, Pickles, in Beulah Hill, south London. Pickles sniffed out a parcel wrapped in newspaper by a hedge. Corbett feared it was a bomb, tore the paper and read "Brazil, West Germany, Uruguay". The police made him their prime suspect and questioned him until 2:30 in the morning before clearing him.

That summer England won the World Cup, and Pickles was invited to the victory dinner, where the players let him lick their plates clean. In December 1983 the trophy was stolen again, from the Brazilian football confederation in Rio de Janeiro. It has never been found.
