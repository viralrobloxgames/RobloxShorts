## The facts (sources below)
- Event: men's marathon, 1904 Summer Olympics, St. Louis, 30 August 1904; 32 starters, 14 finishers (Wikipedia).
- Heat "in the low 90s" F (Smithsonian); about 90 F / 32 C (Wikipedia). Narration: "Over ninety degrees."
- Dust: dirt roads; officials' and doctors' cars drove ahead of the field, raising dust clouds the runners breathed
  (Smithsonian; Wikipedia "dust clouds that exacerbated the severely hot and humid conditions").
- Water: only one official water stop, a well about 12 miles from the start (Smithsonian; Wikipedia "a well at about
  the halfway (12 miles 750 yards)"). The organiser (James Sullivan) limited water on purpose to study dehydration
  (Wikipedia); not narrated.
- Fred Lorz (USA) dropped out about nine miles in and rode in a car for about 11 miles (Smithsonian: "his 11-mile
  ride"); the car broke down around mile 19 and he jogged the rest, crossing the line first (about 3:13). The crowd
  cheered; he was photographed with Alice Roosevelt, who was about to award him the gold (Wikipedia: "she placed a
  wreath upon Lorz's head, and was about to award him the gold medal"), when officials learned about the car. He said
  it was a joke; he was banned for life, later reduced (reinstated 1905) and won the 1905 Boston Marathon.
  Narration: "rode eleven miles in a car."
- Andarín Félix Carvajal (Cuba), a postman: lost his money in New Orleans (a dice game) and hitchhiked to St. Louis;
  ran in a white long-sleeved shirt, long dark trousers (cut at the knee by a fellow Olympian), a beret and street
  shoes (Smithsonian). He chatted with spectators in broken English (St. Louis Post-Dispatch via Smithsonian), took
  peaches from a car, ate apples from an orchard that turned out rotten, lay down and took a nap, and still finished
  fourth (Smithsonian, Wikipedia).
- Len Taunyane ("Len Tau", one of the first two Black Africans at the Olympics) was chased about a mile off course by
  a dog and finished ninth (Smithsonian "chased a mile off course by a wild dog"; Wikipedia).
- Left out of the narration on purpose: the winner Thomas Hicks was given strychnine and brandy by his trainers
  (history only; mention in the post copy at most).

Line 1 says "eleven miles" (not "most of the way": he rode about 11 of the 24.85-mile course).
