The men's marathon at the 1904 Olympics in St. Louis (30 August 1904) might be the strangest race ever run. It was about 90°F, the dirt roads were thick with dust kicked up by officials' cars, and there was just one water stop, a well about 12 miles in. Of 32 starters, only 14 finished.

Fred Lorz of the USA dropped out about nine miles in and rode in a car for about 11 miles. When it broke down around mile 19, he jogged the rest of the way and crossed the line first. The crowd cheered, Alice Roosevelt (the President's daughter) was about to hand him the gold, and then officials learned about the car. Lorz said it was a joke. He was banned for life, though the ban was later lifted, and he won the Boston Marathon in 1905.

Andarín Félix Carvajal, a mailman from Cuba, had lost his money on the way and hitchhiked to St. Louis. He ran in street clothes with his trousers cut off at the knee, stopped to chat with spectators, ate apples from an orchard that turned out to be rotten, lay down for a nap, and still finished fourth. Len Taunyane, one of the first two Black Africans to compete at the Olympics, was chased about a mile off course by a dog and finished ninth.

The official winner, Thomas Hicks, was kept going by his trainers with doses of strychnine (a poison then used as a stimulant) and brandy, which would never be allowed today.
