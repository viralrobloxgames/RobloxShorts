Five hundred and forty-four miles, from Sydney to Melbourne.
World-class runners line up in shiny new gear.
And a sixty-one-year-old potato farmer, in overalls and work boots.
Nobody gives him a chance.

The gun goes off. The pros sprint away.
He shuffles.
By nightfall, he's miles behind.

That night, the pros stop to sleep for six hours.
He wakes up by mistake at two in the morning.
So he just starts running again.
He shuffles past their tents, one by one.
By morning, he's in the lead.

Back home, he'd chase sheep around his farm for days.
So he decides not to sleep at all.
The pros are faster, but they keep stopping. He never does.

Five days later, he shuffles into Melbourne, ten hours ahead of second place.
Nearly two days faster than the record.

They hand him ten thousand dollars.
He gives most of it to the runners he just beat.
