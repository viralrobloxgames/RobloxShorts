## The facts (kept accurate; sources below)
- 1983 Westfield Sydney to Melbourne Ultramarathon, about 875 km (544 miles). Cliff Young, 61, a farmer from Beech
  Forest, Victoria (widely described as a potato farmer; he also ran sheep). He arrived in overalls and work boots
  (Wikipedia; he then ran in a pair of runners someone gave him, and without his dentures). The narration says he
  lines up in overalls and work boots; the picture keeps the overalls (the shoes are not a story point).
- He trailed the pack by a large margin at the end of the first day. The other runners slept about six hours a night.
  He "mistakenly woke up at 2 am, several hours earlier than intended, and began running, taking the lead", then
  decided to avoid sleep and finished the rest of the race without sleeping (Wikipedia). Some retellings say he never
  slept at all from the start; the narration follows Wikipedia's order.
- He had said he used to run for two to three days straight rounding up sheep in gumboots (Wikipedia).
- He finished in 5 days, 15 hours and 4 minutes, 10 hours ahead of the runner-up, almost two days faster than the
  previous record for any run between Sydney and Melbourne (Wikipedia).
- Prize: A$10,000. Accounts differ on the split: Wikipedia says he gave A$3,000 to the runner-up Joe Record and A$4,000
  to other finishers and kept A$3,000; others say he shared it among the five other finishers. Narration: "He gives
  most of it to the runners he just beat", true in both. "Nobody gives him a chance" is narration colour (many accounts
  say he was dismissed as a joke or a spectator; not quoted as fact).
