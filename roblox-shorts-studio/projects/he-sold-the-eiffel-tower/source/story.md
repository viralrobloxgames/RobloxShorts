# He Sold The Eiffel Tower: facts, beats, sources

Standalone true story (one part), overnight run of 5-6 Oct 2026 (no script approval needed tonight; never post
without the user's approval of the finished MP4).

## Facts used (and how the script words them)
- May 1925, Paris: newspapers report the Eiffel Tower is in poor repair and costly to maintain, with talk that it
  might be cheaper to take it down. -> "Newspapers say the tower is rusting, and fixing it costs a fortune."
- Victor Lustig (Czech-born con man, many aliases, called himself "Count") had forged government stationery made,
  as Deputy Director-General of the Ministère des Postes et Télégraphes, and invited a small group of scrap-metal
  dealers (five or six in different accounts) to a confidential meeting at an expensive Paris hotel (often named
  as the Hôtel de Crillon). He told them the tower would be sold for scrap and swore them to secrecy.
  -> "He fakes government letters and invites scrap metal dealers to a fancy hotel." / "It's a secret."
- He picked André Poisson, an insecure dealer eager to rise in Paris business circles; posing as an underpaid
  official, he got a bribe from Poisson as well as the payment (about 70,000 francs in common accounts).
  -> "So Lustig asks him for a little bribe too."
- Lustig left for Austria (Vienna) with the money. Poisson was too embarrassed to go to the police.
  -> "takes a train out of the country" / "too embarrassed to tell anyone".
- Later that year Lustig returned and tried the same con on another group; this time the police were informed and
  he fled to the United States. (Some accounts say he "sold it twice"; the script says he "tries it again", which
  every source supports.)
- In the US he sold "money-printing" boxes, and he conned Al Capone (borrowed $50,000, returned it two months
  later, and Capone gave him a reward). -> "even tricks the gangster Al Capone".
- Arrested for counterfeiting on 10 May 1935 in New York; escaped the Federal House of Detention on 1 September
  1935 with a specially made rope; recaptured 27 days later in Pittsburgh; sentenced to 15 years (Alcatraz) plus
  5 for the escape. -> "caught making fake money, escapes down a rope, and gets caught again."
- Died 11 March 1947 (pneumonia) at the Medical Center for Federal Prisoners, Springfield, Missouri. His death
  certificate gives his occupation as "apprentice salesman" and counterfeiter.
  -> "His death certificate says: apprentice salesman and counterfeiter."

## Cast
- Max: Victor Lustig (top_hat, smug/cool faces; whispering pose).
- Leo: André Poisson (eager, then shocked/embarrassed).
- Mia, Skye: the other dealers; Mia later the dealer who calls the police.
- Noob: the gangster (Capone stand-in, never named on screen beyond the narration) and a police officer
  (officer_cap) in the escape beat.

## Beat plan (about 60 s of speech + 2 s end card)
1. Hook: Max in a top hat hands Leo a big paper deed in front of the tower; "SOLD" stamp. (0-4 s)
2. Paris 1925: newspaper "TOWER RUSTING" flying at camera; rust patches on the tower legs; coins falling.
3. Max reads the paper, lightbulb grin.
4. Typewriter/forged letters with a seal fly out to five dealers.
5. Fancy hotel room, chandelier: Max leans in and whispers; dealers lean in; "TOP SECRET".
6. Leo's eyes sparkle; Max rubs fingers (bribe); Leo slides a bag of cash; a second small bag.
7. Max grabs the cash and boards a train; the train pulls out.
8. Leo alone, embarrassed face, hides the deed behind his back.
9. Max steps back off a train, grins: "AGAIN?!"
10. Mia picks up a phone; police whistle; Max runs, ship to America.
11. Gangster's office: Noob in a dark suit, Max hands back a briefcase; Noob gives him a tip.
12. Money-printing box spitting bills; police burst in; rope out of a window; caught again under a streetlight.
13. Paper close-up: "OCCUPATION: APPRENTICE SALESMAN ... AND COUNTERFEITER".
14. CTA end card.
