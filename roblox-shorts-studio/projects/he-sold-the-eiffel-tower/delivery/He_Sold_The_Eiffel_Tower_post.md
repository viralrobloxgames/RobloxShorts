In 1925 Paris newspapers reported that the Eiffel Tower was in poor repair and expensive to maintain. Con man Victor Lustig saw a chance. He had fake government stationery made, posed as a senior official of the Ministry of Posts and Telegraphs, and invited a small group of scrap-metal dealers to a confidential meeting at an expensive hotel, where he told them the tower was secretly going to be sold for scrap.

He picked André Poisson, a dealer eager to make his name, and got a bribe from him as well as the payment. Then Lustig left for Austria with the money, and Poisson was too embarrassed to go to the police. So Lustig came back and tried the same con again, but this time the police were told and he fled to the United States.

There he even tricked Al Capone, borrowing $50,000 and handing it back two months later to win his trust. In 1935 he was arrested for counterfeiting, escaped from a New York detention centre with a rope, was caught again 27 days later in Pittsburgh and was sent to Alcatraz. When he died in 1947, his death certificate listed him as an "apprentice salesman".
