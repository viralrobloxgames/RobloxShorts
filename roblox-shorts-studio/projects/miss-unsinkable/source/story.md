## Facts used (and how the script words them)
- Violet Jessop (1887-1971) worked as an ocean-liner stewardess from 1908. -> "works on ocean liners".
- 20 September 1911: RMS Olympic, with Jessop aboard as a stewardess, collided with the cruiser HMS Hawke; no one
  died and the Olympic returned to port. -> "collides with a navy warship. Nobody dies."
- April 1912: she sailed on the Olympic's sister ship RMS Titanic as a stewardess (age 24). After the iceberg she was
  put in lifeboat 16, and an officer gave her a baby to look after; survivors were picked up by RMS Carpathia, where a
  woman took the baby. -> "lifeboat sixteen, an officer hands her a baby. She holds it for hours, until a rescue ship
  arrives."
- 21 November 1916: as a Voluntary Aid Detachment nurse she was aboard the hospital ship HMHS Britannic (the third
  Olympic-class ship) when it struck a German mine and sank within 55 minutes; 30 of 1,066 aboard died. Her lifeboat
  was drawn toward the ship's propellers; she jumped and suffered a head injury, and survived. -> "the Titanic's
  other sister", "sinks in under an hour", "pulled toward the giant propellers", "jumps into the sea, hits her head".
- She returned to the White Star Line in 1920, later sailed with the Red Star and Royal Mail lines, and retired in
  1950. -> "keeps working at sea for another thirty years". Nickname: "Miss Unsinkable".
