## The facts (kept accurate; sources below)
- Joshua Abraham Norton came to San Francisco in 1849 and grew rich in trade and property.
- 1852: during a rice shortage he staked about $25,000 on a shipload of Peruvian rice to corner the market; within days
  ship after ship of rice arrived and the price collapsed. Years of lawsuits followed and he was bankrupt by 1858.
- 17 September 1859: he sent a proclamation to the newspapers declaring himself Emperor of these United States
  (he styled himself Norton I; later also "Protector of Mexico"). The San Francisco Daily Evening Bulletin printed it.
- He wore a blue army officer's uniform with gold epaulettes (from officers at the Presidio) and a beaver hat with a
  peacock feather.
- He issued his own notes (Empire of North America scrip, 50 cents to $10), accepted at the places he frequented.
- 12 October 1859: he decreed that Congress was abolished; Congress ignored it (as did the army he ordered to clear it).
- 1867: police officer Armand Barbier arrested him to have him committed; after a public and press outcry, Police Chief
  Patrick Crowley released him with an apology. From then on, police officers saluted him in the street.
- He issued decrees (1869 and 1872 accounts) ordering a bridge across San Francisco Bay; the San Francisco-Oakland Bay
  Bridge opened in 1936 (the narration says only "decades later").
- He died on 8 January 1880. Estimates of the crowds at his funeral run from 10,000 to 30,000 (the narration says
  "thousands").
