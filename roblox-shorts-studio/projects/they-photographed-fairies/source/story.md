## The facts (kept accurate; sources below)
- 1917, Cottingley near Bradford, England: Elsie Wright (16) and her cousin Frances Griffiths (9). Frances kept coming
  back wet from the beck (stream) at the bottom of the garden and said she went there to see the fairies.
- Elsie borrowed her father Arthur's Midg quarter-plate camera; the first photo shows Frances behind a bank with four
  small fairies dancing in front of her. Elsie's father did not believe it (after the second photo he stopped lending
  them the camera).
- 1919-1920: Elsie's mother mentioned the photos at a Theosophical Society meeting; Edward Gardner took them up; the
  photographic expert Harold Snelling found no sign of faking ("genuine, unfaked photographs of single exposure").
- Sir Arthur Conan Doyle, creator of Sherlock Holmes and a spiritualist, used them in his article on fairies in the
  Christmas 1920 issue of The Strand Magazine and treated them as evidence; three more photos were taken in 1920
  (five in all). He wrote The Coming of the Fairies (1922).
- 1983: the cousins admitted the photos were faked: paper cutouts Elsie drew from Princess Mary's Gift Book (1914),
  held up with hatpins. Frances maintained until her death in 1986 that the fifth photograph was genuine; Elsie said it
  was a fake too.
