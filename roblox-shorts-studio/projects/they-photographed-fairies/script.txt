In nineteen seventeen, two girls take a photo of fairies.
Three years later, the man who created Sherlock Holmes tells the world it's real.

Elsie is sixteen.
Her cousin Frances is nine.
Frances keeps coming home wet from the stream.
She says she goes there to see the fairies.

So Elsie borrows her dad's camera.
The photo shows Frances, with tiny fairies dancing in front of her.
Her dad doesn't believe it.

But a photo expert finds no sign of trickery.
And Sir Arthur Conan Doyle believes every word.
He puts the photos in a famous magazine, and writes a whole book about fairies.

For over sixty years, people argue.
Then, in nineteen eighty-three, the cousins finally admit it.
The fairies were paper cutouts, held up with hatpins.

But Frances never gave up on one photo.
Until the end, she said that one was real.
