On 1 April 1957 the BBC's current-affairs programme Panorama, Britain's most serious news show, ran a three-minute report about a family in the canton of Ticino in southern Switzerland harvesting spaghetti from trees.

The respected presenter Richard Dimbleby explained that a mild winter and the "virtual disappearance of the spaghetti weevil" had brought a bumper harvest, and that years of careful breeding had produced spaghetti of a perfect, uniform length. The pickers laid the strands out to dry in the sun.

About eight million people watched. In 1950s Britain spaghetti was still an exotic food that most people knew only from tins of spaghetti in tomato sauce, and the next day hundreds of viewers phoned the BBC, some asking whether it was real and how to grow their own spaghetti tree. The BBC's answer: "place a sprig of spaghetti in a tin of tomato sauce and hope for the best."

It was an April Fools' Day report, dreamed up by the cameraman Charles de Jaeger. Decades later CNN called it the biggest hoax any reputable news organisation ever pulled.
