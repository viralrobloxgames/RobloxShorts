# Spaghetti Grows On Trees (standalone story, one part)

Mode: true story told as grounded action (references/story.md). Hook: on the news, a family picks spaghetti off a tree,
and eight million people are watching. One clear rule: the most serious news show in Britain says it, so it must be
true; every beat adds a straight-faced "fact" (the mild winter, the weevil, the careful breeding) and the viewers'
trust grows until hundreds phone in. Payoff: the deadpan answer ("a sprig of spaghetti in a tin of tomato sauce") and
the date of the show (April the first) reframe the hook. Third person, names nobody. Picked through general research
for the user's overnight run of 2026-10-05 because the named second story (Lawnchair Larry) was already in the ledger as
"He Flew A Lawn Chair"; not in the ledger, not the Emu War or Victor Lustig. Script: `../script.txt` (155 words with the
CTA, one sentence per line).

Cast: Max plays the TV presenter (suit-and-tie look not needed: a desk, a microphone and a serious face); Mia and Skye
the Swiss pickers (Mia on a ladder, Skye with a basket); Leo and Noob viewers at home (armchair, TV set, phone); Leo
also the operator who gives the answer on the phone. Route: web (three.js).

## The facts (kept accurate; sources below)
- 1 April 1957, BBC current-affairs programme Panorama: a three-minute report on a family in the canton of Ticino in
  southern Switzerland harvesting spaghetti from trees (Wikipedia; Museum of Hoaxes). Narration: "Britain's most
  serious news show", "In Switzerland".
- Narrated by the respected broadcaster Richard Dimbleby (Wikipedia). Narration says only "the presenter".
- The report credited a bumper spaghetti harvest to a mild winter and the "virtual disappearance of the spaghetti
  weevil", and explained the uniform length by careful breeding (Wikipedia: "the breeding necessary to develop a strain
  to produce the perfect length").
- The pickers laid the spaghetti out to dry in the sun (the film shows strands draped to dry; Museum of Hoaxes,
  Wikipedia).
- "An estimated eight million people watched the programme" (Wikipedia).
- In 1950s Britain spaghetti "was known mainly from tinned spaghetti in tomato sauce and considered by many to be an
  exotic delicacy" (Wikipedia). Narration: "spaghetti was rare in Britain. Most people only knew it from a tin."
- "Hundreds phoned in the following day to question the authenticity of the story or ask for more information"; some
  asked how to grow their own spaghetti tree (Wikipedia; Museum of Hoaxes).
- The BBC told callers to "place a sprig of spaghetti in a tin of tomato sauce and hope for the best" (Wikipedia;
  Museum of Hoaxes).
- Decades later CNN called it "the biggest hoax that any reputable news establishment ever pulled" (Wikipedia).
  Narration: "it's still called the biggest hoax a serious news show ever pulled."
- The idea came from cameraman Charles de Jaeger (Wikipedia) - post copy only.

## Beats
1. Hook (frame 1): an orchard on a sunny Swiss hillside; Mia on a ladder pulls a long hank of spaghetti off a tree into
   Skye's basket, both faces 3/4 to camera, strands swinging; a 1950s TV frame (rounded screen corners) around the
   shot and a pill "NEWS · 1957". Line 2: pull back to a living room: Leo and Noob in armchairs staring at the TV set;
   a counter "8,000,000 WATCHING".
2. The show: Max at a news desk with a microphone, deadly serious; HUD "BRITAIN'S MOST SERIOUS NEWS SHOW". Then a map
   pop "SWITZERLAND" and a sun with "MILD WINTER" -> "BUMPER HARVEST".
3. Drying: strands draped over a rail in the sun; a cartoon weevil with a red X ("SPAGHETTI WEEVIL: GONE"); Skye holds
   up two strands of identical length beside a ruler ("SAME LENGTH: CAREFUL BREEDING").
4. Back then: a tin of spaghetti on a kitchen shelf, "SPAGHETTI IN BRITAIN" with a tin icon.
5. Phones: next day, Leo and Noob on candlestick phones, a switchboard lighting up, a "CALLS" counter climbing to
   "HUNDREDS"; bubbles "IS IT REAL?" / "HOW DO I GROW ONE?".
6. The answer: the BBC phone operator (Max at the desk, still serious) bubble "PLACE A SPRIG OF SPAGHETTI IN A TIN OF
   TOMATO SAUCE..."; Leo at home literally pokes a strand into a tin of sauce on a pot plant; "...AND HOPE FOR THE BEST."
7. Payoff: a desk calendar flips to APRIL 1; stamp "APRIL FOOL!"; Leo looks at the tin, then at the camera; Max at
   the news desk finally grins. HUD "BIGGEST HOAX EVER PULLED BY A SERIOUS NEWS SHOW".
8. CTA end card.
