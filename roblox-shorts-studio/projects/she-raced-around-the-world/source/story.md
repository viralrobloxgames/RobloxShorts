## The facts (kept accurate; sources below)
- Inspired by Jules Verne's novel Around the World in Eighty Days, Nellie Bly (Elizabeth Cochrane), a reporter for
  Joseph Pulitzer's New York World, proposed going round the world faster than the book's Phileas Fogg.
- Her editor doubted it: she only spoke English, as a woman she would pack too much, and she would need a man to escort
  her; he proposed sending a man instead. Her reply (her own account): start the man, and she would start the same day
  for another newspaper and beat him. Narration: "Her editor says it's a job for a man. She says: start the man, and
  she'll beat him."
- She got two days' notice (told 12 Nov 1889) and sailed on 14 November 1889 at 9:40 a.m. from Hoboken, New Jersey, on
  the Augusta Victoria, with the dress she wore, a sturdy overcoat, a few changes of underwear and a small travel bag
  (and money in a bag round her neck). Narration: "one dress, one coat, and one small bag".
- She met Jules Verne and his wife at their home in Amiens, France, on 22 November 1889.
- Her route went via London, Amiens, Brindisi, the Suez Canal, Aden, Colombo (Ceylon), Penang, Singapore, Hong Kong,
  Yokohama and San Francisco. Narration names the Suez Canal, Ceylon and Singapore.
- Cosmopolitan sent Elizabeth Bisland the other way round (westward) the same day; Bly learned of her only in Hong
  Kong and said she would not race ("If someone else wants to do the trip in less time, that is their concern"). The
  press treated it as a race. Narration: "Now the papers call it a race."
- Rough weather on the Pacific put her into San Francisco two days behind schedule (21 Jan 1890). Pulitzer chartered
  a special train, the "Miss Nellie Bly Special", which covered 2,577 miles in 69 hours.
- She arrived in Jersey City on 25 January 1890 at 3:51 p.m.: 72 days, 6 hours and 11 minutes (a world record, beaten
  a few months later). 80 - 72.26 = 7.74 days: "almost eight days".
- Bisland missed a connection, took a slower ship, and finished in about 76.5 days, four and a half days after Bly.
- Staging only: the editor's office, the faces, the ships and train as built, who drives the train.
