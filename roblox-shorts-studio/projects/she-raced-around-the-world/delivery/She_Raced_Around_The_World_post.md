In 1889, Jules Verne's novel said you could go around the world in eighty days. Nellie Bly, a young reporter at Joseph Pulitzer's New York World, said she could do it faster. Her editor wanted to send a man instead. Her answer: start the man, and she would start the same day for another newspaper and beat him.

On 14 November 1889 she sailed from Hoboken on the Augusta Victoria with the dress she wore, a coat and one small bag. In Amiens, France she met Jules Verne himself. Then the Suez Canal, Ceylon and Singapore. In Hong Kong she learned that Cosmopolitan magazine had sent Elizabeth Bisland around the world the other way, the very same day, and the papers called it a race.

Storms on the Pacific put her two days behind, so Pulitzer hired a special train to race her across America. She reached Jersey City on 25 January 1890: 72 days, 6 hours and 11 minutes, almost eight days faster than the book. Bisland came home four and a half days later.
