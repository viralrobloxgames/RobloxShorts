In 1889, a famous book says you can go around the world in eighty days.
One young reporter says she can do it faster.
Her editor says it's a job for a man.
She says: start the man, and she'll beat him.

Two days later, she sails, with one dress, one coat, and one small bag.

In France, she stops to meet the man who wrote the book.
Then the Suez Canal.
Ceylon.
Singapore.

In Hong Kong, she hears the news.
A magazine sent a rival the other way round the world, the very same day.
Now the papers call it a race.

Storms on the Pacific cost her two days.
So her newspaper hires a special train, and it races her across America.

Seventy-two days.
Six hours.
Eleven minutes.

She beat the book by almost eight days.
And her rival came home four and a half days later.
