## The facts (kept accurate; sources below)
- A little after 1 a.m. on 29 September 2015, Sanmay Ved, a former Google employee (then an MBA student at Babson
  College), searched for google.com on Google Domains, Google's own domain shop. It showed as available; he bought it
  for $12 and the card was charged.
- For about a minute he owned it: messages for the site's owners arrived (Google Search Console / Webmaster Tools
  ownership of google.com) and confirmation emails landed in his Gmail.
- The order was then cancelled and the $12 refunded.
- He reported it to Google Security; Google's Vulnerability Reward Program paid $6,006.13, digits that read like
  "GOOGLE" (6=G, 0=O, 0=O, 6=G, 1=L, 3=E).
- He asked for it to go to charity (the Art of Living India Foundation, which runs free schools in India); because
  he donated it, Google doubled the reward (over $12,000).
- The narration doesn't name him (keeps the hook fast, per story.md); his name goes in the post description.
- Staging only (not claimed as fact): his laugh, the room, the moneybag, the cartoon "squint".
