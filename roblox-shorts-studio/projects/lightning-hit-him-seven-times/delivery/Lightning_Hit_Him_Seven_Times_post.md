Roy Sullivan was a park ranger at Shenandoah National Park in Virginia, and between 1942 and 1977 he was struck by lightning seven times and survived every one.

Strike one (1942) hit him as he ran out of a fire lookout tower with no lightning rod, and took his big toenail. Strike two (1969) bounced off trees and in through his truck window, and burned off his eyebrows. Strike three (1970) jumped from a power transformer to his shoulder in his own front yard. Strike four (1972) set his hair on fire in the ranger station, so from then on he carried a can of water. Strike five (1973): he saw a storm cloud, drove away from it, got out when he thought he was safe, and his hair was on fire again. People started avoiding him; when lightning struck far away, even the chief ranger said "I'll see you later." Strike six (1976) hurt his ankle. Strike seven (1977) hit him while fishing, and a bear came to steal the trout off his line; he chased it off.

Guinness World Records still lists him for the most lightning strikes survived: seven.
