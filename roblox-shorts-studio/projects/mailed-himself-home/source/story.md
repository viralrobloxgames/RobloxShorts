# He Mailed Himself Home (standalone story, one part)

Mode: true story told as grounded action (references/story.md): open inside the stunt (the lid being nailed down),
one clear rule (he is cargo now, so he goes wherever cargo goes and gets treated like cargo), each beat changes the
danger or who knows (fog, the Bombay tarmac, the shed in Perth, the friend who never heard back), and the ending
reframes the opening: he got in the box because he couldn't afford a ticket, and in the end he never paid for one.
Third person; the athlete is never named in the narration (keeps the hook fast). **A true story, found through
general research, not from OddFrame's list** (user request 2026-10-05: "Make another story").

Why this one: frame 1 reads on mute (a face peeking out of a crate as the lid goes on), it is cartoon-safe (nobody
hurt, no crime beyond a dodged freight bill that was waived), it has built-in comedy (the "one for later" bottle, the
paint label, upside down in the sun, walking out of an airport in a suit, forgetting to text your friend), a warm
motive (his daughter's birthday), and a neat reversal at the end.

Script: `../script.txt` (160 words with the CTA, one sentence per line). Expect ~62-65 s at George's pace. If the
take runs long, cut "Inside: " -> drop the pillow/torch list to "Inside: two bottles.", then "in the burning sun".
Cast: Max plays the athlete (javelin on his back in the opening, a suit for Perth); Leo is the friend with the hammer;

## The facts (kept accurate; sources below)
- Reg Spiers, 22, an Australian javelin thrower from Adelaide, went to England in 1964 hoping to make the Olympic
  team for Tokyo. He didn't (accounts differ: an injury, failing to qualify), and he was broke (some accounts say his
  wallet with his savings was stolen). He wanted to be home for his daughter's birthday. Narration: "an athlete,
  broke in London"; it does not say Olympian or why he was broke.
- He was working in the export cargo section at Heathrow and had seen animals shipped cash on delivery: "if the dogs
  can survive it, I could" (Criminal podcast).
- His friend John McSorley, a British javelin thrower, built the box: about 5 ft x 3 ft x 2.5 ft, so he could sit
  upright or lie with knees bent, with straps to hold himself steady. McSorley nailed/closed him in on 17 October 1964.
- Packed: tinned food, a torch, a blanket, a pillow, and two plastic bottles, one for water and one for urine
  (Yahoo News AU; BBC summary). "One for later" is the narration's polite version. (One AP caption says "without food or
  water"; the detailed later accounts all list the food and bottles.)
- Labelled as paint, sent cash on delivery (the recipient pays the freight on arrival) to a non-existent shoe
  company / a fictitious "Mr Graham" in Perth (AP 1964 caption; Yahoo; Wikipedia summary). Air India cargo flight.
- Fog at Heathrow delayed the flight (accounts say 20, 24 or 28 hours). Narration: "for hours", true of all of them.
- Stops included Paris and Bombay; at Bombay the box was left on the tarmac for hours in the heat, at one point upside
  down, and he stripped off his clothes in the heat (Yahoo; BBC summary). The narration says "upside down" and "burning
  sun"; the picture shows him sweating in his vest, never undressed.
- 63 hours after London, he arrived in Perth (about 20 Oct 1964), was put in a cargo shed, cut his way out of the box,
  put on his suit and walked out of the airport unchallenged, then hitchhiked about 1,700 miles (2,700 km) home to
  Adelaide, in time for his daughter's birthday. His wife didn't believe him at first.
- He forgot to tell McSorley he had arrived safely; McSorley assumed the worst, panicked and called a journalist, and
  the story went around the world (Lethbridge News Now / AP 2017 retelling).
- The airline waived the cost: "the airline forgave his debt" (Atlas Obscura) / "was pressured into allowing him to fly
  for free" (Lethbridge News Now). Narration: "the airline drops the bill", and the payoff "he never paid for one".
- Shown in post copy only: Spiers and McSorley lost touch for decades and met again in 2016; their story is told in
  "Out of the Box" by Marcus and Julie McSorley. Not in the narration or post copy: his later drug-smuggling
  conviction (off-tone and not needed).

## Beats
1. Hook (frame 1): a London garage at night; Max sits folded inside an open wooden crate, face 3/4 to camera,
   nervous grin; Leo leans over with the lid and a hammer (both faces read, no back of a head). Bang: the lid drops,
   one nail goes in. "THIS WAY UP" stencil on the side.
2. Inside (torchlight close-up): Max clicks on a torch; pans across a pillow, a stack of tins, two plastic bottles,
   one labelled "WATER", the other with a blank label; Max looks at the second bottle, then at the camera. Beat.
3. Backstory insert: Max with a javelin on an English field, then turning out empty pockets (a moth flies out);
   a calendar with a birthday cake drawn on a date, a photo of a small kid in Australia.
4. Mailing himself: a hand-written address card slides into frame and slaps onto the crate.
5. Label: close-up stencil "PAINT"; a tag "C.O.D." stamped red; zoom on the address: a shoe company with a big "?"
   flickering over it (the company doesn't exist). Leo and a cargo handler (Skye) push the crate on a trolley (real walk).
6. Fog: grey fog rolls over a Heathrow apron; the plane waits; a clock spins; inside, Max taps his fingers.
7. Bombay: hot orange light, heat shimmer; the crate sits upside down on the tarmac; inside, Max hangs upside down
   from his straps, sweating, the pillow on his face; ground crew (Noob) walks past fanning himself.
8. Perth: "63 HOURS" counter ticks up as the plane lands; a dark cargo shed; a saw blade pokes through the crate wall
   and cuts a square; Max climbs out side-on (fits through the hole), straightens a suit and tie.
9. Walks out: Max strolls past a sleepy guard and out through the airport doors into sunshine (real walk); then on a
   desert road with his thumb out; a truck stops (one-arm wave).
10. The birthday: a front door in Adelaide; Mia opens it, stares (shock); the kid runs in; a cake with candles; Max
    `proud`. Music swells.
11. The friend: back in London, Leo pacing by a silent phone, clock ticking; he grabs the phone and dials;

12. Whole world knows: newspapers spin in: "MAN POSTS HIMSELF HOME", "63 HOURS IN A BOX"; a TV in a shop window.
13. The bill: an airline clerk (Skye) holds an invoice stamped "C.O.D.", looks at the newspaper, then tears the bill
    in half (or a big "PAID: NOTHING" stamp).
14. Payoff: Max at home with his family, the empty crate in the yard used as the kid's playhouse; text "TICKET HOME:
    NEVER PAID FOR". Hold long enough to register.
