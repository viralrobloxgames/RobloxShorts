## The facts (kept accurate; sources below)
- 1961: the Norwegian King's Guard (Hans Majestet Kongens Garde) performs at the Edinburgh Military Tattoo; Lieutenant
  Nils Egelien takes an interest in Edinburgh Zoo's penguins.
- 1972: back in Edinburgh, the regiment adopts a king penguin, named Nils Olav (after Nils Egelien and Olav Siggerud),
  and makes him a visekorporal (lance corporal).
- Promotions on later visits: corporal 1982, sergeant 1987, regimental sergeant major 1993, honorary regimental sergeant
  major 2001, colonel-in-chief 2005; knighthood 15 August 2008 (approved by King Harald V; 130 guardsmen paraded at the
  zoo; the citation called him "in every way qualified to receive the honour and dignity of knighthood"); brigadier 22
  August 2016; major general 21 August 2023.
- A 4-foot bronze statue of Nils Olav was presented to Edinburgh Zoo in 2005 (the narration places "his own bronze
  statue" after the knighthood with "even", not a date).
- The first Nils Olav died in 1987 shortly after his promotion to sergeant; a two-year-old near-double, Nils Olav II,
  took the name and rank; Nils Olav III has held it since 2016 (Guinness: highest-ranking penguin).
- "Soldiers who trained for years still have to salute him": soldiers salute superior officers; at the inspections the
  guard presents arms to him. Played as the reframe, not a regulation.
