On 2 July 1982, Larry Walters, a truck driver from Los Angeles, sat in a lawn chair tied to 42 helium weather balloons in a backyard in San Pedro. The plan was to rise about 100 feet on a rope tied to his Jeep. The rope broke, and he shot up to about 16,000 feet, where it was freezing.

He had wanted to fly since he was a boy, but his poor eyesight meant he could never be a pilot. He took sandwiches, jugs of water and a pellet gun to pop balloons when he wanted to come down. Pilots of TWA and Delta airliners radioed air traffic control that they had passed a man in a lawn chair.

He shot a few balloons and started to sink, then dropped the gun. His cables caught a power line in Long Beach and the neighbourhood lost power for about 20 minutes. He landed unhurt, and the police were waiting. Asked why he did it, he said: "A man can't just sit around."

The FAA fined him for flying an aircraft without talking to the control tower. Today his lawn chair is in the Smithsonian's National Air and Space Museum.
