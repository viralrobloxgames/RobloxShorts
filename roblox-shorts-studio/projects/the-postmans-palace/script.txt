A postman trips over a stone and almost falls.
He doesn't kick it away. He puts it in his pocket.

It's 1879, and that strange stone reminds him of an old dream: a palace.

So on his thirty-kilometre mail round, he starts picking up stones.
First in his pockets. Then in baskets. Then he brings a wheelbarrow.

At night, by an oil lamp, he builds.
The whole village laughs at him.

He keeps going.
Twenty years, just for the outer walls.
Then animals, giants and towers.

Thirty-three years later, it's finished.
Ten thousand days. Ninety-three thousand hours.
On the wall he writes: "Let those who can do better, try."

He asks to be buried inside it. The law says no.
So he builds his own tomb too. Eight more years.

Today his palace is a protected monument.
And the village that laughed is famous because of him.
