## The facts (kept accurate; sources below)
- Joseph Ferdinand Cheval (19 April 1836 - 19 August 1924), rural postman (facteur) at Hauterives, Drome, France.
- 1879, aged 43: on his round "my foot tripped on a stone that almost made me fall"; a stone "of such a strange shape
  that I put it in my pocket to admire it at leisure" (his own account, quoted on Wikipedia). It reminded him of a
  dream of building a palace (official site / tourism sources).
- His round was about 30 km a day (official site, facteurcheval.com). He gathered stones on it: first in his pockets,
  then in baskets, then with a wheelbarrow (Wikipedia, official site).
- He worked at night by the light of an oil lamp (Wikipedia). Neighbours ridiculed him (several sources: "despite
  ridicule from his neighbours"). Narration: "The whole village laughs at him."
- The first twenty years went on the outer walls (Wikipedia). The palace mixes animals, giants and towers
  (sculpted animals, three giants, the Barbary Tower; staging only beyond those words).
- Built 1879-1912: 33 years. Inscription on the palace: "1879-1912 10000 days, 93000 hours, 33 years of struggle. Let
  those who can do better try." (Wikipedia; the official site quotes "10,000 days, 93,000 hours, 33 years of
  hardship"). Narration: "Ten thousand days. Ninety-three thousand hours." and the "do better" line.
- Size (staging): about 26 m long, 10-12 m high (Wikipedia, tourism sources).
- French authorities would not let him be buried in the palace; he spent eight more years building his own mausoleum,
  "The Tomb of Silence and Endless Rest", in the Hauterives cemetery, and died about a year after finishing it
  (Wikipedia; official site).
- 23 September 1969: classified a historic monument, backed by Minister of Culture Andre Malraux (French Wikipedia,
  ICI/France Bleu). Narration: "a protected monument".
- "The village that laughed is famous because of him": the palace is Hauterives' landmark and a major tourist site
  (tourism sources); stated as a reframing, without visitor numbers.
- Narration names nobody; Ferdinand Cheval, Hauterives and the Palais Ideal go in the post description.
