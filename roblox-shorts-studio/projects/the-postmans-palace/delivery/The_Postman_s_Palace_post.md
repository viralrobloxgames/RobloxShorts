In 1879 a French country postman, Ferdinand Cheval, tripped over a stone on his round. It had such a strange shape that he put it in his pocket, and it reminded him of an old dream: building a palace.

So he started collecting stones on his round of about 30 km a day: first in his pockets, then in baskets, then with a wheelbarrow. He built at night by the light of an oil lamp, and his neighbours laughed at him. The outer walls alone took 20 years; then came animals, giants and towers.

He finished in 1912, after 33 years. On the wall he carved: "10,000 days, 93,000 hours, 33 years of struggle. Let those who can do better try." He wanted to be buried inside it, but the law said no, so he spent eight more years building his own tomb in the village cemetery.

In 1969 the Palais Idéal in Hauterives, France, was classified a historic monument, and the village that laughed is now famous for it.
