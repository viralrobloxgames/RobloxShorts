In 1896, a man in England gets the world's first ever speeding charge.
His speed? Eight miles an hour.

The limit in town is two.
By law, a car needs three people to run it, and one of them has to walk out in front.

He has no walker. He just drives.
Through the village, at four times the limit.

So a policeman jumps on his bicycle and gives chase.
Five miles later, he catches him.

In court, the driver is charged with four things at once.
Driving with no horse. Not enough crew. Speeding.
And no name and address on his car.
Guilty, guilty, guilty, guilty.

Ten months later, the law changes.
No more walker. The new speed limit? Fourteen.
Drivers celebrate with a run from London to Brighton.
And people still drive it, every single year.
