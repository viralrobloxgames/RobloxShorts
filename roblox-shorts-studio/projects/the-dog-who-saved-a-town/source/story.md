## The facts (kept accurate; sources below)
- January 1925: Dr Curtis Welch found a diphtheria outbreak in Nome, Alaska. The port had frozen over in November, so
  no fresh antitoxin could come by ship until spring. Narration: "Kids are getting sick" (diphtheria mostly struck
  children); "Ships can't get through the ice."
- Planes: Alaska's few biplanes had open cockpits and water-cooled engines, unfit for the deep cold, and officials
  chose dogs. Narration: "Planes can't fly in that cold."
- The serum came by train to Nenana; from there to Nome is 674 miles. Narration: "nearly seven hundred miles away".
- About 20 mushers and around 150 dogs relayed it (sources say "more than 100" to "about 150"). Narration: "Twenty sled
  teams will pass it along, like a relay."
- Leonhard Seppala's lead dog Togo was 12 years old. Seppala ran about 170 miles east from Nome to meet the serum near
  Shaktoolik, then about 91 miles back to Golovin: 261 miles in all, by far the longest leg (most legs were 25-50
  miles). Narration: "a hundred and seventy miles just to reach it ... then turn around and race back"; "Two hundred
  and sixty-one miles... more than any other team."
- On the way back he took the shortcut across the ice of Norton Sound in a blizzard (around -30 F, gale winds, wind
  chill near -85 F), with Togo finding the way to the roadhouse at Isaac's Point. "While they slept, it had blown out to
  sea all the ice Seppala had just crossed." Narration: "Togo takes a shortcut across a frozen sea, in a blizzard. That
  night, the storm blows all that ice out to sea. They crossed it just in time."
- The relay took 5 days and 7.5 hours (127.5 hours) and reached Nome at 5:30 a.m. on 2 February 1925; not one vial
  broke, and by 3 February the epidemic was under control. Narration: "Five and a half days later, the medicine
  arrives, and the outbreak is stopped." (Official deaths 5-7; "stopped" = brought under control.)
- Gunnar Kaasen drove the final 53 miles (Bluff to Nome) with Balto in the lead. A statue of Balto by Frederick Roth
  was unveiled in Central Park, New York, on 15 December 1925. Narration: "the dog who ran the last fifty-three miles
  gets the statue in New York. His name was Balto."
- Togo's first statue: Seward Park, Manhattan, 2001 (76 years later). Narration: "Togo had to wait seventy-six years
  for his."
- Staging only: faces, the town, the sled, the plane, the hospital room, who plays whom; the dogs are Roblox-style.
