# Competitor teardown: Mia Rants — 6 October 2026

Channel: [@MiaRantsRoblox](https://www.youtube.com/@MiaRantsRoblox/shorts). 132K subscribers, 60 Shorts and one long video, 3.5M lifetime views. Our numbers for comparison are in [analytics-log.md](analytics-log.md).

## Bottom line

1. **Their story Shorts are not why they get more views.** Every format they post lands at about 20K to 30K: 42 of 60 Shorts sit between 15K and 36K. The 11 narrated stories (the ones like ours) got 13K to 29K and none broke out. Stories are 7% of the channel's views.
2. **The channel was built by a different format.** "Which X Do You Choose?" Shorts are 88% of lifetime views. Eight of them (115K to 779K) are 62% on their own, and 89% of the subscribers arrived by 16 May, before a single story was posted.
3. **The 20K to 30K level belongs to the channel, not to each video.** Sloppy videos and careful ones get the same views, the first video after a two-month gap got 23K, and a video identical in template to its 20K+ siblings stalled at 1.4K. Their subscribers are not the source: the long video got 216 views from 132K subscribers.
4. **What is worth copying is how they open a story**, because the first 2 seconds is our known weakness: narration from frame 0, the whole premise in a first-person sentence that is also the title, the threat or payoff already on screen, a cut to a close-up by 1 to 2 s and a red circle at about 2.5 s.
5. **We cannot see their retention**, so we can't say whether their stories hold viewers better than ours. Judge our own tests by "stayed to watch", not by their view counts.

## What was checked

- Public view counts, like counts, lengths and publish times for all 60 Shorts, read from YouTube on 6 October 2026 in a signed-out browser.
- 34 Shorts and the long video were watched by five sub-agents through timestamped frame sheets (0.5 s steps for the first 3 s, then every 2.5 to 3 s) and YouTube's auto-captions. Nobody heard the audio, so voice type and music are not assessed.
- Channel history from the About page, the only Wayback Machine capture (29 April 2026), Social Blade (12 to 16 May only) and the Discord invite.
- Not available: retention, traffic sources, audience countries, anything before 29 April.

"Inferred" below marks a reading of the evidence, not something observed.

## Where the views come from

- **The floor does not track quality or topic.** The lantern video (1 Sep, 1.4K) uses the same script template, the same like and subscribe lines and the same kind of images as four siblings that got 20K to 36K. The three videos stuck at about 1,300 views share no format with each other. Like rates on the flops (5.7 to 9.2%) are close to normal, so the people who saw them reacted normally. Inferred: those videos stalled at the first test audience and were never widened.
- **The floor does not track subscribers.** It was the same at 42.8K subscribers as at 132K. The long video reached 0.16% of subscribers in its first day or two.
- **The floor survives gaps.** The first Short after breaks of 22, 65 and 24 days finished at 28K, 23K and 26K.
- **Like gating moves likes, not views.** Stories with a like gate get 43 to 62 likes per 1,000 views against 7 to 30 without, with views in the same band.
- **Open question: did the channel have this reach on day one?** The first upload shows 26.8K, but the earliest snapshot is 29 April, a week after the 779K video. The first four videos may have been lifted afterwards. Most likely reading (inferred): one breakout on the fifth upload set the channel's level with the Shorts feed, and nearly everything since is shown to a similar-sized audience.

## Format 1: "Which X Do You Choose?"

**The template (Golden vs Diamond version, 28 to 35 s, 150 to 190 words, about 5 words a second):**

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0 to 0.5 s | Roblox obby gameplay, avatar seen from behind, "Yo bro" |
| 0.5 to 2 s | Caption "Yo Bro, Golden X, or Diamond X 🤔"; an image box flips meme, gold item, diamond item |
| 3 to 12 s | Golden perk with three examples, one caption and one clip every 2.5 s |
| ~10 to 16 s | "The only catch: you have to like this video right now to get it" |
| 12 to 22 s | Diamond perk with three examples, then "subscribe right now" |
| ~22 to 28 s | "But wait, there's a hidden black X. This one gives you both." |
| Last 3.5 s | "To unlock it, like, subscribe and share this video in the next 3 seconds." Loops straight to the hook. |

- Nothing is unlocked and there is no payoff. Like rates are 7 to 14%.
- The image box is filled with third-party clips: anime, a cat meme, Patrick Star, American Psycho, Death Note, iShowSpeed GIFs. The same clip sits on the same script beat in every video.
- **Superpower items did best:** glasses (X-ray) 287K, notebook (what you write comes true) 264K, shoes 234K, gum (mind reading) 202K. Lifestyle items did worse: car 160K, school 115K.
- **The 779K video is an earlier, simpler version:** two Roblox avatars on blue and yellow panels (a nice "emo girl" and an annoying "acorn head"), 25 s, 117 words, one like-and-subscribe line at the end. It is the most Roblox-native one and uses a real dilemma with a twist.
- **The one that beat the template on content** was an original scenario (24 Apr, 88K): a week off school if you deep-clean one of three nightmare rooms, with a hidden VIP room. It is the only video with a comment ask.
- Inferred production cost: 30 to 60 minutes each once the template exists.

## Format 2: narrated stories

Staged inside Roblox with ordinary avatars, one narrator avatar ("Mia", brunette in a black ushanka) in every video, one reused guard avatar who plays guard, manager and dad, and one school map reused across videos. New shot every 2 to 2.5 s in the "Secretly Lived" videos, one caption line per shot.

### How every story opens

Seen in all five "I Secretly Lived…" videos:

| Time | Picture | Words |
|---|---|---|
| 0.0 s | Wide shot, the threat (guard) walking toward camera | Narration starts on frame 0: "I secretly lived inside my school…" |
| ~1 to 2 s | Cut to a close-up of the narrator peeking out of her hiding place | "…and the guard had no idea." |
| ~2.5 s | Red hand-drawn circle (sometimes an arrow) on her face | |
| ~3 s | Back to the wide shot | Caption: "And the guard had NO idea." |

- The first spoken sentence, the first caption and the title are the same sentence.
- The "In This World…" videos open on the payoff instead: money or Robux is already raining at frame 0, with a "+1,000,000" pop-up and a red circle at 2 to 4 s.
- Their weak spot: no caption for the first 0.1 to 0.5 s, and the first second is a distant wide shot with tiny characters.

### "I Secretly Lived…" template (57 to 65 s, 123 to 147 words, 2.1 to 2.3 words a second)

| Time | Beat |
|---|---|
| 0 to 6 s | Hook as above |
| ~6 s | "Every night after closing, the whole X was mine." |
| 10 to 20 s | Three perks, each in a new location |
| 16 to 24 s | "Until one night…" something goes missing |
| 25 to 35 s | Guard finds evidence, a reward is offered, a friend snitches |
| 33 to 51 s | Chase: slide under his legs, wet floor, crash |
| 41 to 47 s | Like or subscribe gag (three of the five) |
| Last 5 to 10 s | "…and found FOLTYN living in there. Since 2015!" Ends on a reaction close-up. No loop, no end CTA. |

Three of the five (school, mall, school all summer) share about 70% of their beats. Supermarket and boyfriend's house are the more original two and got the same views.

### "In This World…" template (42 s, about 84 words, 2.0 words a second)

Rule and prize in the first sentence, a three-step escalation ($1,000, $10,000, a million), the guard tries to take it, like gate, the guard is punished by the rule itself, subscribe gate, then "It was CRUZ the whole time". The two gates take about 12 s, 30% of the runtime.

### The like gate

A fake heart button with "1.2M" under it appears on screen at about 40% of the runtime: "Click LIKE so he can't steal my million. Three… two… one… Phew." A red badge counts 3, 2, 1 and the heart fills. A "Subscribe" pill does the same at about 72%: "Subscribe to see who made this rule."

| | Likes per 1,000 views | Views |
|---|---|---|
| Stories with a like gate (4) | 43 to 62 | 17K to 26K |
| Stories without one (7) | 7 to 30 | 13K to 29K |

The 13K video was a day old when counted. Two of the seven have a subscribe pill but no like gate.

### Other things seen

- **Cameo names:** the twist reveals Foltyn, Cruz, Snowz, Steak, Clanker and Caylus. Inferred: these are other Roblox YouTubers, used for recognition.
- **No comment mechanic:** 4 to 13 comments per story, no question to the viewer, empty descriptions.
- **Mistakes they get away with:** narration says "50, then 80, then 200" while the screen shows 57, 107, 230; "I was under the bed" over a shot of a ladder; a caption typo "YOBROW"; night lighting that turns to day mid-scene.
- **Titles carry five hashtags** ("#roblox #shorts #rblx #robloxstory #relatable"). That weakens our earlier guess that hashtag-heavy titles held back our first two uploads.

## What to test

Their identical-template videos range from 1.4K to 36K, so one video's views prove nothing. Compare "stayed to watch" and the TikTok 2-second drop across at least three videos.

1. **Rebuild the first 3 seconds to their pattern, and go one better.** Narration and caption both on at frame 0; first sentence is the whole premise and is also the title; threat or payoff already moving in frame 0; close-up of the lead by 1 s; red circle or arrow at about 2 s.
2. **First-person premises a child understands from the title alone.** "I secretly…", "In this world…", "I found a…". Everyday settings: school, mall, supermarket. One fixed narrator avatar.
3. **Move the ask into the story at about 40%**, tied to a stake, with a 3-2-1 countdown. Expect more likes (about five times in their data), not more views.
4. **Try three Roblox-native "which would you pick?" Shorts on YouTube only.** This is the format that built their channel. Use our own renders of both options on colour panels, as their 779K video did, superpower-type options, and a real payoff at the end. They would be about 30 s, so they don't qualify for TikTok Creator Rewards.

## What not to copy

- "Like, subscribe and share in the next 3 seconds to unlock it" with no payoff. It is plain engagement bait; the policy risk is inferred, not checked.
- Third-party anime, film and meme clips. We have already had one Content ID block.
- Re-skinning one script daily. Three near-identical stories in a row is their main weakness.
- Long-form fan fiction of licensed anime.
- Other creators' names as twist reveals, unless we decide that deliberately.

## Not established

- Views of the first four uploads before the 22 April breakout.
- Why Social Blade stops at 16 May with a "banned or deleted" banner, the same day a 22-day upload gap begins. The channel is live.
- 14 May shows +14.9K subscribers on +73K views in Social Blade. Probably a lagging view counter; not evidence of anything else.
- Whether the voice is text-to-speech.
- Whether the cameo names are real creators' avatars or only their names.
