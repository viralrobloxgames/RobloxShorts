# Roblox horror video research — 2 October 2026

## Recommendation

Test an original, recurring Roblox horror series built around **one impossible event, one understandable rule and a payoff in every episode**. Start with an empty-server mystery using Max, Mia and Leo; add a night-shift set second. Keep character humour, but let the threat remain real within the fiction. A joke should release tension without making every episode turn out to be a prank.

This is a production recommendation, not a claim that original server horror is currently the largest category. The strongest recent public examples verified here are Forsaken fan scenarios and DOORS animation. Night-shift comedy and forest survival have substantial proven examples, with weaker evidence of a fresh October surge.

The previous chat **“Roblox horror series research”** proposed The Night Shift, The 1-Player Server and Don't Blink. Those were suggestions, not an approved series or script. No horror entry or dedicated plan existed in the local idea ledger when this audit began. This report preserves that direction and adds evidence and an actionable asset plan.

Read next: [series and pilot plan](horror-series.md) and [asset build checklist](../assets/roblox_pack/HORROR_ASSETS.md).

## What was checked and what the numbers mean

- Searched current and older Roblox horror, animation, Shorts, night-shift/anomaly, Forsaken, DOORS and forest-survival examples. Used a September recency filter for current leads.
- Opened original YouTube pages in a signed-out browser to check public counts, durations and dates for key examples; examined Davebb933's separate Videos and Shorts tabs. Read the available night-shift Short transcript. This was a metadata/format review, not a complete viewing or shot-by-shot analysis of every video.
- Counts below are cumulative public views observed on **2 October 2026**, unless a secondary-source snapshot is explicitly stated. They are not last-24-hour views, unique viewers, retention, qualified TikTok views or earnings.
- This is a purposive sample, not a platform-wide ranking. Established channels, upload age, fandom recognition, language and format all confound comparisons. No reliable public competitor RPM or audience-country data was available.
- TikTok examples were available through third-party indexing, not verified live in TikTok. They are supporting leads only. Claims about what is working *right now* rely primarily on recent YouTube examples.

## Themes worth using

| Theme | Evidence strength in this sample | Transferable story device | Production consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Familiar characters in “what if” encounters | Strong recent examples | State a specific conflict immediately; let the audience predict the outcome | Establish our cast and one repeatable entity; avoid a large monster roster |
| A normal job with an abnormal customer | Strong older example; current momentum uncertain | Show normal behaviour, then one visible violation | Small service window, shutter, rule card, monitor and two versions of a customer |
| Noob versus survival threat | Strong historical Shorts examples, some newer demand | Vulnerable hero, clear objective, resource running out | Forest kit later: fire, torch, cabin, safe boundary and one threat |
| Game rules that betray the player | Promising fit, not directly proven as October's leading format | Player count contradicts the picture; chat replies before typing; leave button fails | Mostly reusable UI, an empty lobby and controlled duplicate actors |
| Monster personality and character comedy | Supported by horror-setting skit and animation packaging | Surprise through behaviour, not merely a louder jumpscare | Expressions and acting matter more than complex creature geometry |
| Long mythology / 3AM / old Guest legends | Historical reference only in this review | A mystery can link episodes | Do not start with lore explanations or claim an invented threat is real |

My inference is that **recognition plus a concrete situation** is a safer starting point than generic “scary Roblox story” packaging. Rewatchable clues, a legible rule and a twist are creative hypotheses to test; public view counts cannot prove they caused retention. September results support continuing interest in horror fandoms, not a proven Halloween uplift.

## What the channel's own history adds

The local [posting analysis](posting-schedule.md), dated 2 October, records **£74.89 and 1.23M views for “Scariest Roblox error code”**, and only £0.50 so far for the new story Shorts. These are inherited internal results, not a fresh analytics pull in this research. The error-code result supports testing eerie game-interface premises. It does not prove that an animated fiction version will earn similarly.

The same internal review records 33.5 seconds average watch time and roughly 40% completion for Admin Part 1, versus 22.6/21.6 seconds and 4–8% completion for later parts. Upload ages were unequal. Use it as a reason to make every horror pilot understandable by itself, not as proof that all sequels fail.

Keep the existing **22:30 Europe/London story slot** for the initial experiment, so we do not simultaneously change format and timing. Treat the schedule as a test: it cannot guarantee US distribution or high RPM, and correlations in that document do not establish causation. Evaluate countries and actual rewards rather than assuming an English voice or spelling change selects the audience.

## What to test

1. Make three standalone, approximately 65–75-second originals under one provisional series identity. Each has a complete immediate payoff and a small recurring clue. Start with the empty server; then test duplicate-player horror and the night shift.
2. Keep cast, narration voice, duration range, caption treatment and story slot broadly consistent. Preserve a self-contained opening even if the cover has an episode number.
3. Log at 24 hours, 72 hours and seven days: views, average watch time, completion, shares per 1,000 views, follows per 1,000 views; on YouTube also stayed-to-watch and engaged views; on TikTok also qualified views, estimated rewards, RPM if available and audience geography. Use matching measurement ages for comparisons.
4. Working decision rule, not an industry benchmark: continue a premise when at least two pilots beat the median of the latest five comparable originals on watch time and shares, without a clear completion collapse. Compare money after seven days. If reach is too small for a meaningful comparison, record “inconclusive” and improve the opening before expanding assets.
5. Track production hours too. A new genre that doubles work for no retention or earnings improvement is not yet a useful daily format. Build forest or custom creature rigs only after a pilot provides a reason.

TikTok's [official programme explanation](https://support.tiktok.com/en/business-and-creator/creator-rewards-program/how-is-the-creator-rewards-program-different-from-the-tiktok-creator-fund?invalid_lang=hi) says qualifying content must be original, high quality and longer than one minute; length alone does not guarantee eligibility or payment. The 36-second viral example is a creative reference, not a Creator Rewards template. YouTube distinguishes public playback counts from [engaged views used in its reporting](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9082582?hl=en), so use Studio metrics for our tests rather than copying competitors' headline totals.

## Scope and remaining uncertainty

This research produced planning documents only. No models, narration, renders, purchases or uploads were made. No current TikTok-wide horror ranking, competitor retention or competitor earnings was verified. The prior chat's precise live game rankings, broad claims about monetisation and platform strictness are not adopted here. A game-player count measures gameplay demand, not the performance of its videos.
